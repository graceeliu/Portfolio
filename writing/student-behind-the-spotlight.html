The Student Behind the Spotlight

Category
Journalism
Form
Reported profile
Published
UCSB Humanities & Fine Arts, March 2023
Context
Digital Journalism, Writing Program

A profile of a fourth-year Theater and Dance student who was the lighting designer for two UCSB productions running that March, written for my Digital Journalism course and published by the UCSB Humanities and Fine Arts student journalism site.

The reason I wanted to write it is in the subject itself: lighting design is a role that becomes invisible when it's done well. The audience notices a spotlight following an actor and attributes the effect to the performance. So the piece had to make a technical craft legible to readers who had never thought about it — including the division between lighting design, which is an aerial plot of where every instrument goes plus a cue placed against the script, and technical lighting, which physically realizes that plan. In an educational theater the same person often does both, which is not how the professional world works.

The reporting also turned up what makes it a story rather than a description. She was one of very few lighting students in the department, which meant unusual access to large productions as an undergraduate — and the show she had adapted was about to tour five countries.

Structurally it's a straightforward profile: open on the moment the work is visible, then go behind it. The discipline was resisting the urge to explain everything. A reader needs enough technical detail to understand why the craft is hard, and no more.

About

I started in cognitive psychology and neuroscience, studying how people recognize faces, form impressions, and remember them — first through behavioral experiments for my undergraduate honors thesis, then with fMRI and behavioral data for my master's research at Columbia.

That training turned out to transfer cleanly to product work. The questions are the same: how do people categorize what's in front of them, what do they attend to first, and what happens when the system's model of a task doesn't match the mental model of the person doing it. The difference is that a wrong answer on a clinical floor has consequences, so the research has to hold up.

I work across the full range: semi-structured field interviews, card sorting, diary and observational studies, pre-registered experiments with decision rules set in advance, survey design, and quantitative analysis in Python and SQL. I build my own instruments when the off-the-shelf ones don't fit, and I write the memos and dashboards that get findings in front of people who make the call.

I'm looking for product research, UX research, human factors, and HRI roles. I'm most drawn to problems where the interface sits between a person and a decision that matters — health and wearables, payments and fintech, and anything with hardware in the loop.

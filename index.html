About me

I've always been a bit of an explorer, whether in health, writing, or data, and that range is what makes me adaptable.

I started in cognitive psychology and neuroscience, studying how people recognize faces, form impressions, and remember them. That began with behavioral experiments for my undergraduate honors thesis and continued with fMRI for my master's research at Columbia.

That training transferred cleanly to product work, because the questions are the same. How do people categorize what's in front of them? What do they attend to first? What happens when a system's model of a task doesn't match the mental model of the person doing it? I'm especially curious about how small things, like a word choice or a micro-decision, shape behavior. On a clinical floor a wrong answer has consequences, so the research has to hold up.

I work across the full range, from field interviews and card sorts to pre-registered experiments and quantitative analysis, and I build my own instruments when off-the-shelf ones don't fit. Underneath all of it is something I care about: making science and information accessible. There's something for everyone. The real challenge is making that first connection.

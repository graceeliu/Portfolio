Memorial Sloan Kettering: general vs. domain-specific LLMs on medical questions

Both the general and the domain-specific model wrote answers that sounded right, and a standard similarity metric scored them almost identically. Only the metrics that checked facts could tell the outputs apart. The medical-domain model hallucinated less and covered more of the expert answer, but neither was close to safe to use unsupervised.

TLDR

The question
LLMs write fluent answers that can be factually wrong. Does a medical-domain model answer medical questions more accurately than a strong general-purpose one?
What we did
The plan was to fine-tune a model on clinical notes, but without enough GPU access we pivoted to a head-to-head test: Llama 3.1 (general) vs. Asclepius (medical), both 8B and 4-bit quantized, on 141 hand-verified medical questions, scored three different ways.
What we found
A standard similarity metric couldn't tell the models apart. Fact-checking metrics could: Asclepius hallucinated less and covered more of the expert answers, but neither was safe to use unsupervised.

What it means

  • Fluency hides the gap. On semantic similarity, the models tied. Relying only on that metric would conclude domain training adds nothing.
  • Domain training helped where it counts. Asclepius led on every metric that checked facts.
  • Neither model is ready to stand alone. Even the better one hallucinated in more than one answer in five. For clinicians and patients, a plausible-but-wrong answer is the dangerous failure, so evaluation has to check facts directly.
